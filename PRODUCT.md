# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Homeowners and vehicle owners across the Phoenix metro area (Chandler, Scottsdale, and the greater East Valley) who search for mobile car detailing — typically "car detailing near me" on their phone, often via a paid Google Search ad — with high, immediate purchase intent. They're deciding whether to let a stranger detail their car at their own home or office, so trust matters more here than in a typical local-service purchase: who's showing up, whether the car is really insured against damage, and whether the "5 stars" claim is real.

## Product Purpose

Convert local search intent into booked mobile detailing appointments. The site's job is to prove trustworthiness fast (licensed & insured, a named owner-operator who does every job himself, real reviews, real before/after results) and remove friction between "I want my car detailed" and "it's booked" — ideally through instant self-serve pricing and booking rather than a delayed callback promise.

## Brand Personality

Clean, direct, trustworthy. Plain-spoken and transparent — no hidden fees, pay-after-the-job stated upfront, real prices shown before anyone has to ask. Confident without being showy. Personal rather than corporate-anonymous: the owner, Jordan, does every job himself, and that fact is a genuine differentiator, not marketing copy.

References the user pointed to (shwash.co, cleanmobiledetail.com) both land in the same territory already implied by the brand: a blue-family accent on white, real transformation photography of technicians and vehicles (not stock imagery), guarantee language stated plainly ("100% satisfaction guarantee"), and named/verifiable reviews at volume. These aren't a pivot from the current design — they're validation to lean further into the direction already underway (before/after photo gallery, licensed/insured trust strip, expanded real reviews).

## Anti-references

- Generic templated local-service site look (stock hero gradient, interchangeable-with-any-competitor structure).
- Corporate/anonymous detailing chains that erase the personal owner-operator angle — losing "it's always Jordan" to make the brand feel more like a franchise would undercut the actual differentiator.
- Glossy, aspirational luxury-supercar-detailing aesthetic (black/gold, unattainable) — this is a grounded, everyday-car, local service, not a premium/exotic detailing brand.

## Design Principles

1. **Prove trust before asking for anything.** Licensed & insured, real reviews, before/after photos, and "always Jordan, never a sub" need to be visible on arrival, not buried in an FAQ accordion.
2. **Remove the wait.** A visitor with active purchase intent should be able to see a real price and book instantly. The callback form is a fallback for people who want to talk first, not the default or co-equal path.
3. **Show, don't just claim.** Transformation photography and named, verifiable reviews carry more weight than adjectives — every trust claim on the page should have visible backing behind it.
4. **Local and personal over corporate.** The differentiator is a real person doing every job himself; design choices should reinforce that human, local feel rather than chain/franchise polish.
5. **One primary action per page.** Instant booking is the default path everywhere; secondary actions (call, request a callback) are visually subordinate.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline: sufficient color contrast, full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, labeled and autocomplete-enabled forms, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion`. This matches the fixes already applied this session to `landing-page-chandler-redesign.html` and should be the standard going forward for every page, not a one-off pass.
